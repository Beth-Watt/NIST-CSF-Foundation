# NIST-CSF-Foundation-README.md
What happens when an Orientation &amp; Mobility Specialist takes a cybersecurity course

## A Little Background

I came into this course from an unconventional background as an Orientation & Mobility Specialist who has been building a foundation in the cybersecurity space. I teach people who are blind or visually impaired how to move safely and independently through the world - schools, neighborhoods, transit systems, places they've never been before.

I enrolled in this course expecting to learn something that had nothing to do with my day job. That's not what happened.

## The Aha Moment - I've Been Doing GRC This Whole Time

It didn't hit me all at once. It built the whole way through the course until I couldn't unsee it.

First connection came early, NIST is flexible by design because no two organizations have the same cybersecurity needs. Even similar businesses in the same industry can't just copy each other's approach. That's my students. Two kids, same diagnosis, completely different functional vision. Same framework, totally different application. That landed immediately.

Then the risk assessment piece. NIST requires an independent assessment before anything else, figure out where you are, find the gaps, build a plan. That's exactly what I do before I write a single IEP goal. Functional vision assessment, O&M assessment, learning media assessment. You assess first because you can't build a plan around assumptions. The assessment drives everything that comes after.

Then the gap analysis - Current Profile, Target Profile, prioritize where the distance between the two is greatest. I do this without calling it that. The biggest gaps in a student's independence and safety get addressed first. Can't safely cross a street? That comes before bus travel. Highest risk, most immediate attention.

Then stakeholder reporting. Assessment findings have to be translated and presented to executives so they understand the risk and can authorize resources. That's an IEP meeting. That's me sitting across from a principal and a parent explaining in plain language what the assessment found and what the student needs. Same exact dynamic.

By the end I realized that the assess, educate, analyze cycle that NIST runs on is the same cycle I've been running my whole career. I just never had that language for it.

## What the Course Actually Covered

### NIST CSF
The framework itself - six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. The point is that it's not a checklist. It's a structure you apply based on your specific risk context. Two organizations can use the same framework and end up with completely different implementations. That flexibility is the whole point.

### NIST SP 800-53
The control catalog. This is where the specifics live - 20 families of security and privacy controls that federal agencies use to protect their systems. Controls are tiered Low, Moderate, High depending on how sensitive the system is. The goal across all of it is confidentiality, integrity, and availability - keeping information protected, accurate, and accessible to the right people.

Controls fall into three buckets consisting of operational (people and processes), technical (the actual systems and tools), and management (governance and oversight). All three have to work together.

### NIST SP 800-53 and SP 800-37 Together
800-53 is the what. 800-37 is the how. SP 800-37 is the Risk Management Framework - a seven step lifecycle for selecting, implementing, and managing controls over time. Prepare, Categorize, Select, Implement, Assess, Authorize, Monitor. It treats security as something you maintain continuously, not something you set up once.

That lifecycle maps almost exactly to the IEP cycle. Assess the student, determine the level of need, select the goals, deliver instruction, measure progress, get team sign off, keep monitoring. Same structure.

### The Multi-Tier Approach
Risk management operates at three levels consisting of the organization, the business process, and the individual system. Decisions at the top have to connect all the way down, and information from the bottom has to flow back up. Nothing operates in isolation.

### Assess. Educate. Analyze.
Strip everything down and that's what it is. Assess where you are. Educate and implement to close the gaps. Analyze what's working and adjust. Then do it again. Not a project with an end date, an ongoing cycle.
